# DEC-24 — How much can we bend before it isn't the same instrument?

*Formal name: the cellular budget. Cite this record as **DEC-24**.*

**Status:** open; no owner assigned · **Who decides:** the project owner · **Kind:** standing obligation — unlike every other record here, this one never closes; it gets an owner and a ledger

> **In one sentence:** five properties make this platform the specific instrument it claims to be; four of them are currently up for negotiation, each for good reasons, one case at a time — and nobody is watching the total.

---

## What this is about

An instrument is defined by its constraints. A knife that gains a motor, a guard, and a trigger has become something else — maybe something useful, but knife-handling rules no longer cover it, and everyone who was told "it's a knife" now holds wrong expectations.

This platform's identity — the reason its results are interpretable the way they are — rests on five constraints:

| The constraint | Where it stands |
|---|---|
| Participants are simple, bounded things | **Held.** The bound on what a participant may carry is law and not tunable. |
| One rule applies everywhere | **In play** — DEC-1 asks whether several rules may run at once. |
| Everything updates at the same moment | **In play** — DEC-3 asks whether we offer richer clocks. |
| Interaction is with neighbours only | **In play** — DEC-21 asks whether declared long jumps count as local. |
| Every participant is the same kind of thing | **In play** — DEC-22 asks whether worlds may declare several kinds. |

Four of five, under simultaneous negotiation. And here is the uncomfortable part: **every single relaxation on that list is well argued.** Real fire spots. Real wards hold patients and staff and rooms. Real incidents live in timing gaps. Each request arrives from an honest Lab with a legitimate need, gets its own record, and is weighed on its own merits.

That's the failure mode. Not one bad decision — a series of individually reasonable ones, after which the platform is a general-purpose simulator wearing a cellular-automaton nameplate, and nobody can point to the moment it stopped being what it said it was. The external review of our core documents recommended relaxing three of the four and never mentioned the total — not carelessness; evidence that **the total goes unwatched by default.**

## What this record actually asks for

Not a decision in the usual sense. Two standing things:

1. **A floor.** A written statement of what remains true of *every* experiment on this platform regardless of how DEC-1, 3, 21, and 22 land. The floor is what lets anyone say "this is still ruliology" and mean something checkable.
2. **A ledger rule.** Any future proposal to relax one of the five must state its effect on the total, not just its own merits — and Labs are already required to report their relaxation requests here (the Lab requirements bind them to it).

## What would make this easy to start

> **Write the floor first, before deciding DEC-1, 3, 21, or 22.** It's easier to say what must survive before the negotiations than after them — and it gives each of those four decisions the constraint they're currently missing.

## What this is blocking right now

- Nothing mechanically. Everything philosophically: DEC-1, 3, 21, and 22 are each being weighed without knowing what the aggregate is allowed to cost.

## The precise version

*This is the wording other documents cite.*

How much of what makes this platform recognisably a local-mechanism instrument may be spent, who keeps the ledger, and what is left when it is empty. Five characterising properties: discrete cells; bounded local state (held — CELL-5, not tunable); one uniform rule everywhere (DEC-1); simultaneous update (DEC-3); interaction only between neighbours (DEC-21); with kind-uniformity of participants (DEC-22) the same class of spend. SCR-F §45.12 asks reviewers to hunt precisely this over-generalisation and gives them nothing to measure against. Labs report relaxation requests into this record (`../01-core/labs.md` LAB-16).
