# DEC-4 — What does "confident" mean here?

*Formal name: Study inference discipline. Cite this record as **DEC-4**.*

**Status:** open; stance constrained · **Who decides:** the project owner · **Kind:** boundary — the style is settled; the threshold and its owner are not

> **In one sentence:** when a Study answers a question — or admits it couldn't — somebody's standard of "good enough to say so" was applied, and we have not yet said whose standard that is or where it lives.

---

## What this is about

A Study runs an experiment many times and reports what happened. Our house style for that report is already settled, and it reads like this:

> *Nineteen of twenty starting positions produced a travelling shape. The one that did not started inside the obstacle.*

Counts. What was varied. The exception, described. What was *not* tested, stated right next to what was. Anyone — a nurse, an investigator, an engineer — can read that and know exactly how much weight it holds.

What is **not** settled: one of our recorded failure types is "the evidence did not answer the question with the required confidence." Required by whom? Set where? If a Study reports 12 of 20, is that an answer, a failure, or a "run more"? Somebody has to own that bar.

## What's already decided

The style constraint is firm and written into the Study requirements: plain counts, proportions, and side-by-side comparisons; no number that can't be traced to a computation; no implied precision the method doesn't have; and **no significance figures against unstated comparisons** — the professional statistics bodies themselves warn that those numbers are routinely misread even by researchers, and our readers are practitioners, not statisticians.

So the decision is not *whether* to be modest and plainspoken. That's law. The decision is who sets the bar and where it's recorded.

## Your options

### A — The asker sets it, per Study
State up front: "I'll call this robust at 18 of 20." Honest and flexible; but most people won't know what to ask for, and bars set after seeing results are worthless.

### B — The Lab sets it, per kind of question
A Lab says what its field considers adequate for each Study pattern. Puts the judgment where the domain knowledge is; but requires every Lab to do statistical homework, and Labs will vary in quality.

### C — The platform sets a floor, Labs and askers may raise it
A minimum below which a Study always reports "not answered," with Labs setting stricter bars where their field demands it. One consistent floor; but a platform-wide number is exactly the kind of false precision the style rules warn about.

## What would make this easy to decide

> **When a Study fails to answer, who do you want explaining why — the person who asked, the Lab that framed it, or the platform?**

Accountability and bar-setting should live in the same place. Whoever must defend the call should be the one who made it.

## The precise version

*This is the wording other documents cite.*

What "required confidence" means, who requires it, and what a Study is permitted to claim. Constrained by SCR-F §20.3 and `../01-core/studies.md` STUDY-7 through STUDY-10: modest, plainspoken statistics — counts, proportions, distributions, paired comparisons, in ordinary language, with what was not tested stated alongside what was. Significance theater, invented precision, and untraceable confidence numbers violate F-5 and §43 at once (Wasserstein and Lazar, 2016 — cited in `../01-core/studies.md`).

**A decision here owes:** the alternatives considered; the reasoning in plain language; what evidence would reopen it; the documents to be revised; and a status change recorded rather than assumed.
