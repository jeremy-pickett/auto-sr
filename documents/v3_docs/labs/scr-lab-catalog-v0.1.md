## 0. What this document is, and what it is not

Sixty candidate Labs, named and briefly described. A Lab is where a domain enters the platform and where it is held accountable (SCR-F §11, §29).

**Presence in this catalog is not fit.** Under SCR-F §30, a Lab earns its place by answering nine questions — domain fit, World fit, mechanism fit, time fit, evidence fit, accuracy, failure boundaries, comparison to established tools, and transfer limits — and a Lab may fail that review. Nothing here has been through it. This is a menu of candidates, written so the fit reviews have something to be run against.

**Standings shown in brackets are inherited, not new.** Seventeen of these carry a verdict from the position paper's eighteen graded verticals, with its reasoning and precedent behind them: **[strong]**, **[plausible]**, **[weak]**, **[insane]**. The eighteenth, retail foot-traffic layout, is dropped — it graded weak-to-insane for the same reason as parking lots, and one boundary marker of that kind is enough. The other forty-three entries carry nothing, and their silence is honest — no one has done the work. A downstream document must not promote an ungraded entry by writing confidently about it.

**Two known gaps.** First, the information security family (H) is entirely ungraded despite being the most likely commercial lead; my expectation is that it depends heavily on non-grid Layouts (SCR-F §15) and would grade weak if forced onto a lattice, but that is a guess and the fit reviews own it. Second, economic and organizational domains are nearly absent. That is deliberate rather than an oversight: on the position paper's own criteria they tend to fail the same way opinion dynamics does, and Family I retains the boundary markers instead of padding the catalog with more of them.

Families are an organizing convenience. They are not a proposed package structure and they are not Layout assignments.

## Family A — Fire, land, and surface processes

**1. Wildfire Lab** [strong]
Fire spread across terrain where each Cell holds fuel, moisture, slope, and burn state, and ignition passes locally under wind. The most established CA domain in operational use, which makes it the platform's natural calibration anchor.

**2. Smoldering and Peat Fire Lab**
Slow subsurface combustion that can persist for months and re-emerge far from the original front. Interesting to SCR precisely because visible surface quiet does not mean the computational state has stopped — the hidden-state case SCR-F §38.6 already names.

**3. Landslide and Debris Flow Lab**
Slope failure propagating downhill as loaded material destabilizes its neighbors. Cells carry saturation, cohesion, and accumulated load; the question is whether local failure cascades or arrests.

**4. Dune and Ripple Lab** [plausible]
Wind-driven sand transport producing regular ripple spacing and migrating dune forms. Werner-class lattice models are established precedent, so this Lab mostly tests whether generated mechanisms rediscover known ones.

**5. Coastal Erosion Lab**
Shoreline retreat, barrier island breaching, and sediment redistribution under wave and storm forcing. The tick has an awkward meaning here — a storm event and a decade of ordinary drift are not the same duration — which makes it a good stress test of SCR-F §30.4.

**6. River Braiding Lab**
Channel migration, bar formation, and avulsion in sediment-loaded rivers. Local erosion and deposition rules produce global channel networks that reorganize abruptly.

**7. Karst Dissolution Lab**
Chemical dissolution of soluble rock producing conduits, sinkholes, and cave networks. A slow-mechanism, long-horizon domain where the interesting structure is the void rather than the material.

**8. Permafrost Thaw Lab**
Thermokarst development where local thaw changes drainage and albedo, which accelerates neighboring thaw. A feedback-driven front problem with obvious policy relevance and equally obvious calibration difficulty.

## Family B — Ice, water, and atmosphere

**9. Sea-Ice Melt Pond Lab** [plausible]
Meltwater pond geometry on Arctic ice, including the percolation transition where ponds connect and drain. Pond fraction drives albedo, so the emergent geometry is the quantity that matters.

**10. Snow and Crystal Growth Lab** [plausible]
Dendritic crystal morphology under local vapor and temperature conditions. Gravner–Griffeath models set a high bar; the honest risk is producing gorgeous images that add nothing to established work.

**11. Cellular Convection Lab**
Open- and closed-cell cloud patterns arising from local convective overturning. A rare case where the real phenomenon is already visibly cellular from orbit.

**12. Vegetation Banding Lab**
Tiger-bush and other regular vegetation stripes in semi-arid landscapes, where plants concentrate runoff locally and starve their downslope neighbors. A well-studied self-organization case with real desertification stakes.

**13. Plankton Patchiness Lab**
Bloom formation and patch structure from local growth, grazing, and mixing. The vertical dimension and advection are the parts a lattice will handle badly, and the fit review should say so early.

## Family C — Ecology

**14. Invasion Ecology Lab** [strong]
Colonization fronts and patchy establishment as a species spreads into new habitat. Cells carry occupancy and suitability; the question is whether spread is frontal, leapfrogging, or stalled.

**15. Forest Gap Dynamics Lab**
Canopy gap formation from treefall and the successional scramble that follows. Multi-decade horizons with a clear local-competition mechanism.

**16. Pest Outbreak Lab**
Bark beetle and similar outbreaks where local host density, tree stress, and beetle pressure determine whether an infestation stays endemic or erupts. The endemic-to-epidemic threshold is the phenomenon of interest.

**17. Coral Reef Competition Lab**
Space competition among corals, algae, and grazers, including regime shifts from coral- to algae-dominated states. Local overgrowth rules producing a bistable landscape.

**18. Mycelial Network Lab**
Fungal foraging that grows, thickens useful paths, and abandons unproductive ones. Sits awkwardly between a growth process and a network optimizer, which makes it a useful Layout boundary case.

**19. Seed Dispersal and Recruitment Lab**
Where seeds land, survive, and establish, given local dispersal kernels and habitat quality. Distinct from invasion ecology in that the mechanism of interest is reproductive rather than frontal.

## Family D — Cells, tissue, and disease

**20. Biological Pattern Formation Lab** [strong]
Spots, stripes, banding, and shell pigmentation from local activation and inhibition. Deep established literature and the cleanest available demonstration that simple local rules produce specific recognizable form.

**21. Excitable Media Lab** [strong]
Spiral waves, wave break, and re-entry in cardiac and other excitable tissue. Greenberg–Hastings-class models are established, and the clinical question — what makes re-entry start and persist — is genuinely open.

**22. Cortical Spreading Depression Lab**
Slow depolarization waves across cortex, implicated in migraine aura and injury progression. Another excitable-media case with a different timescale and different failure consequences.

**23. Avascular Tumor Growth Lab** [plausible]
Margin morphology and necrotic core formation in tumors before vascularization. Local nutrient limitation and proliferation rules; the fit review must be blunt that this is morphology, not prognosis.

**24. Wound Healing Lab**
Epithelial sheet closure where cells migrate, divide, and stop on contact. A closing hole is a front problem with an unusual property — success is the disappearance of the structure being studied.

**25. Biofilm Morphology Lab** [plausible]
Branching, fronting, and sectoring in bacterial colonies under nutrient gradients. Well-established experimental comparison images make this unusually testable.

**26. Immune Response Lab**
Local immune cell recruitment, pathogen clearance, and the granuloma-style structures that form when clearance fails. Cells here are literal cells, which is either clarifying or a trap.

**27. Cell Sorting and Tissue Boundary Lab**
Differential adhesion causing mixed cell populations to sort into layers and sharp boundaries. One of the oldest lattice-model results in developmental biology.

## Family E — Materials

**28. Corrosion Pitting Lab** [plausible]
Pit initiation, front roughening, and percolation through a corroding surface. Local electrochemistry abstracted into neighbor rules; the abstraction gap is wide and should be stated.

**29. Dendritic Solidification Lab**
Dendrite formation as a melt freezes under local undercooling and solute rejection. Directly determines cast-metal microstructure and therefore material properties.

**30. Grain Growth Lab**
Grain boundary migration during annealing, where large grains consume small ones. A classic Potts-model domain with well-characterized statistics to check against.

**31. Fracture Propagation Lab**
Crack initiation and path selection through heterogeneous material under load. Local stress redistribution is the mechanism; whether a lattice can carry stress honestly is the fit question.

**32. Sintering Lab**
Particle bonding and densification during heating, including pore closure and shrinkage. The interesting structure is again the void space rather than the material.

**33. Thin-Film Growth Lab**
Deposition, surface diffusion, and island coalescence producing film roughness and continuity. Local landing-and-hopping rules with direct manufacturing relevance.

**34. Battery Dendrite Lab**
Lithium plating and dendrite growth during charging, the mechanism behind a class of cell failures. Same geometry as crystal growth with substantially higher stakes when it goes wrong.

**35. Catalytic Surface Reaction Lab**
Reaction patterns on catalyst surfaces, including the oscillating and spiral structures seen in CO oxidation on platinum. Established CA precedent and directly observable experimental patterns.

## Family F — Movement and crowds

**36. Crowd Egress Lab** [strong]
Clogging arches, lane formation, and density waves at exits. Operationally used in egress design, which makes it a second calibration anchor alongside wildfire.

**37. Highway Traffic Lab**
Phantom jams, stop-and-go waves, and capacity collapse on roads. Nagel–Schreckenberg-class CA are the canonical demonstration that a trivially simple local rule reproduces a real and counterintuitive phenomenon.

**38. Pedestrian Flow Lab**
Counterflow lane formation, corner congestion, and bottleneck behavior in ordinary walking spaces. Distinct from egress in that nobody is panicking and the layout is the variable.

**39. Warehouse Robot Lab** [plausible]
Deadlock and corridor congestion among coordinated robots in a fixed layout. Diverges from classical CA because the agents have a scheduler, and where that scheduler lives is a real design question.

**40. Degraded-Information Evacuation Lab**
Evacuation where signage fails, exits are unknown, or information reaches people late and unevenly. The interesting variable is what agents can observe, which makes it a direct test of SCR-F §18.5 observation staleness.

## Family G — Cities and infrastructure

**41. Urban Growth Lab** [strong]
Edge growth, leapfrog sprawl, and corridor development. SLEUTH is calibrated on historical land-use maps and in operational planning use, giving this Lab an unusually concrete accuracy benchmark.

**42. Power Grid Cascade Lab**
Cascading failure as tripped lines redistribute load onto neighbors. A network-Layout domain where the phenomenon is fast, rare, and catastrophic — a poor match for lattices and a good match for SCR-F §15's Network World.

**43. Water Distribution Lab**
Contaminant spread and pressure loss through pipe networks. Physical flow constraints make this less abstractable than it first appears, which the fit review should test hard.

**44. Freight and Rail Congestion Lab**
Yard congestion, cascading delay, and network-wide gridlock from local capacity limits. Delay propagates along fixed routes with schedules, which strains the meaning of one tick.

**45. Service Cascade Lab**
Cascading failure in microservice architectures, where retries and timeouts turn one slow dependency into total outage. Retry storms are a local rule producing a global failure, and the domain has excellent telemetry for comparison.

**46. Routing Instability Lab**
Route flapping and reachability loss as routing state propagates between neighboring systems. Genuinely a network domain with a protocol attached, and whether the protocol survives abstraction is the whole question.

## Family H — Information security

*Ungraded as a family. These are the entries most likely to require Identity, Network, or Agent Layouts (SCR-F §15) rather than grids, and forcing them onto a lattice is the failure mode to watch for.*

**47. Lateral Movement Lab**
An attacker moving host to host across a network as credentials, trust, and reachability permit. Cells are hosts or identities and Connections are what actually allows a hop, which makes this the flagship test of non-grid Layouts.

**48. Identity and Privilege Lab**
How permission accumulates through role membership, delegation, and inheritance, and how privilege becomes reachable through paths nobody designed. The domain's structure is a relationship graph, not a space.

**49. Prompt Injection Lab**
Injected instructions propagating through systems where models read attacker-influenced content and act on it. Local contamination spreading along a trust topology that the system's designers rarely drew.

**50. Agent Memory Lab**
Contaminated or drifted memory persisting and spreading among cooperating agents. Hidden state that outlives the visible interaction, which is exactly the pattern-versus-computational-state distinction SCR-F §38.6 protects.

**51. Sensitive Data Diffusion Lab**
Copies of sensitive data spreading through shares, exports, backups, and downstream systems until nobody knows where it lives. The mechanism is mundane and local; the outcome is the part organizations get wrong.

**52. Ransomware Spread Lab**
Encryption propagating across reachable file shares and hosts, and where containment actually bites. Time matters more here than in most Labs, since the defender's response is racing the mechanism.

**53. Patch Propagation Lab**
How updates actually roll out across a fleet, including stalled cohorts and long tails. The defensive mirror of worm propagation, running on the same topology with different incentives.

**54. Worm and Botnet Lab**
Self-propagating code spreading by scanning and exploitation. Historically the domain that made everyone reach for epidemic models, and the place to test whether those models mislead.

**55. Segmentation and Containment Lab**
Whether a network division actually stops propagation, and which single connection defeats it. This is Study-shaped rather than Run-shaped — the interesting output is a comparison, not a trajectory.

**56. Software Supply Chain Lab**
Compromise propagating downstream through dependency and build relationships. A directed acyclic graph rather than a lattice or a mesh, which makes it a distinct Layout case from lateral movement.

## Family I — Weak fits and boundary markers

*Retained deliberately. A catalog with only defensible entries hides where the platform's boundary is, and SCR-F §30 treats a failed Lab as useful evidence.*

**57. Epidemic Spread Lab** [weak]
Wave speed and cluster size in disease transmission. Contact networks are not lattices and the wrong topology produces wrong wave speeds, so any grid version is qualitative only and must say so loudly.

**58. Opinion and Adoption Lab** [weak]
Consensus domains, stable minorities, and adoption cascades. Voter models are established, but the real influence structure is a social network wearing a grid costume.

**59. Ant Trail Lab** [weak]
Trail formation and decay through pheromone deposition. Weak in pure-CA form because the agents move and the field remembers, which is closer to two coupled mechanisms than one — a natural test case for DEC-1.

**60. Parking-Lot Lab** [insane]
Occupancy waves as drivers choose spaces. Retained as the catalog's boundary marker: drivers decide using global information — entrance location, visible vacancy across the whole lot — so a local-rule model of it is a diorama, not a mechanism.

## Open items

1. **Nothing here is fit-reviewed.** Sixty candidates, nine questions each, is the work this catalog creates rather than the work it does.
2. **Family H needs grading against the position paper's criteria before any commercial positioning leans on it.** If those Labs grade weak, that is a finding about SCR's boundary and should be published as one.
3. **Several entries strain the meaning of one tick** — coastal erosion, karst, freight, forest gaps — and time fit (SCR-F §30.4) may be the criterion that eliminates the most candidates.
4. **Several entries imply more than one mechanism** — ant trails, evacuation with degraded information, warehouse robots, vegetation banding. They are blocked on DEC-1 and should not be built first.
5. **No entry here has a Layout assignment.** Family grouping is thematic; SCR-F §15's four Layout families cut across it, and a Lab may need one that does not exist yet.
