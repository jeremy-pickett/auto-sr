## What this family is, and why it is the hard one

Family A was physical systems where locality, geometry, and forcing could usually be pointed at directly. This family is different, and the difference is the point:

> **The hard question here is rarely whether local interaction exists. It is whether the chosen level of description is honest.**

Cellular convection *looks* cellular and is generated by continuum fluid dynamics. Plankton patchiness *looks* spatially patterned and is substantially advected rather than biologically self-organized. Melt ponds have a genuine percolation transition whose statistics are already owned by universality. Snow crystals have the catalog's most beautiful output and its weakest practical need.

Two principles follow, and both are reusable well beyond this family.

**Morphological resemblance is evidence about outcome, not mechanism.** A honeycomb cloud deck looks like a cellular automaton and that proves almost nothing. Establishing a mechanism match needs transition statistics, response to controlled change, persistence and recovery behaviour, and Reader-measured features beyond the final picture. This is precisely where Studies become worth more than screenshots.

**Abstraction level is itself a fit question.** Saying "the World is a grid" is not enough. What one Cell *represents* decides whether the mechanism is local at all — and in this family the same domain gives different answers at different levels. Every entry below therefore declares its level explicitly and states what disappears at that level.

**A note on references.** Items marked **[V]** were checked against a primary or authoritative source; **[D]** are described generically and are background, not citable claims. The verification pass corrected the first pass in three places recorded below.

## Lab 9 — Sea-Ice Melt Ponds

| | |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Role** | Architecture stress test (connected-region constraint) · mechanism-supply (narrow) |
| **Catalog status** | Standalone Lab |
| **Standing** | [plausible], inherited; not re-derived |
| **Abstraction level** | A patch of ice surface, metres across. *Lost at this level:* the vertical ice column and the ablation physics beneath a pond. |
| **Falsifiable question** | What local rules reproduce pond morphology when the ice substrate itself evolves and drainage can reset the system? |
| **Mechanism fit** | Good, with a named architectural dependency |
| **Validation class** | **Direct observational** — hundreds of thousands of measured ponds |
| **Rediscovery risk** | Medium |
| **Practical need** | Real; small field, already occupied |
| **Reach** | **Connected-region constraint** — the defining case |
| **Topology** | Fixed |
| **Drivers** | Melt forcing as **external input**; ice ablation under ponds as **interactive mechanism** |
| **Visual credibility** | Class 3, rising to Class 1 if framed as albedo feedback |

**The phenomenon.** Arctic sea ice melts from the top; meltwater collects in depressions. Ice is bright, ponded water dark, so a pond warms, deepens, and grows while surrounding ice stays bright. Pond fraction is a dominant control on summer Arctic solar absorption. Early ponds are small and round; later they elongate, connect, and — once connected to the floe edge or a drainage hole — drain abruptly, resetting albedo.

**The established shortcut.** Area–perimeter data from hundreds of thousands of ponds shows an unexpected separation of scales: pond **fractal dimension transitions from 1 to 2 around a critical area of 100 m²** [1]. This is read as a percolation transition. A two-dimensional **random field Ising model** follows directly, identifying ponds as metastable states and exhibiting a second-order transition from isolated to clustered ponds [2].

**Reducible.** Albedo from pond fraction. Flooded area from fixed topography and water volume — a level-set calculation with no dynamics. Percolation threshold and near-critical size-distribution scaling: **universal exponents, tabulated in advance.** That last point cuts hard against the Lab — if the phenomenon is a percolation transition, reproducing its statistics demonstrates only membership in a universality class (framework §1.2).

**Irreducible.** The **evolving substrate** — ice topography is not fixed; ponds ablate the ice beneath them, so occupation probability becomes a function of the pattern's own history, which is exactly what static percolation assumes away. Drainage events, resetting the system on a threshold crossing over a heterogeneous field. Refreezing lids, which change albedo without changing pond extent.

**The architectural point this Lab owns.** Water finds level: a connected pond has one surface elevation throughout, however large. No strictly local rule computes that. The framework's helper boundary decides what is permissible — **equalizing a quantity across a connected component is plausibly a generic execution primitive; a melt-pond hydrology solver is a subject answer smuggled into the core.**

**Cell state.** *Persistent:* ice thickness or surface elevation, water depth, frozen-lid state. *Derived:* albedo, drainage connectivity, pond membership.

**Assessment.** *(judgment, no standing)* The opening is narrow and specific — the co-evolving substrate, which percolation universality does not cover — and the first pass was right to attack its own value proposition. Keep; it is the family's cleanest test of the helper boundary.

## Lab 10 — Snow and Crystal Growth

| | |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Role** | Integrity demonstrator · rediscovery benchmark |
| **Catalog status** | Standalone Lab (benchmark-only in practice) |
| **Standing** | [plausible], inherited; not re-derived |
| **Abstraction level** | A lattice site in a growing crystal. *Lost at this level:* surface attachment kinetics, which is where the open physics actually sits. |
| **Falsifiable question** | Which local attachment and noise mechanisms reproduce measured sidebranch statistics or degree of six-fold symmetry? |
| **Mechanism fit** | Superb |
| **Validation class** | **Direct experimental** — controlled-chamber growth at known conditions |
| **Rediscovery risk** | **High** |
| **Practical need** | **Very low** |
| **Reach** | Neighbour-local |
| **Topology** | Fixed |
| **Drivers** | Vapour field as **interactive mechanism** — the crystal depletes what it reads |
| **Visual credibility** | **Class 2 — beauty is the failure mode** |

**The phenomenon.** A protruding tip intercepts more diffusing vapour than a flat face, grows faster, and protrudes more. That instability produces branches, and repeated, sidebranches — across plates, columns, needles, sectored plates, capped columns, and dendrites.

**The established shortcut.** The **Nakaya morphology diagram** maps habit against temperature and supersaturation: plates and stars near 0 to −3 °C, needles and columns from −3 to −10 °C, plates at low supersaturation and branched forms at high from −10 to about −22 °C [3]. **Mullins–Sekerka** (1964) gives the linear stability condition for a growing interface, and solvability theory shows crystalline anisotropy selects a unique tip operating point — without anisotropy there is no dendrite, only unstable fingers [12]. **Diffusion-limited aggregation** [13] is the minimal lattice model of the same instability.

**The cellular precedent is exceptional.** **Gravner and Griffeath (2009)** built a three-dimensional mesoscopic model on vapour diffusion, anisotropic attachment, and a boundary layer, faithfully replicating most observed snow-crystal morphology [4].

**Correction from the first pass.** "Read the diagram" oversimplified. The Nakaya diagram is a strong empirical guide for crystals growing in air near 1 atm [3], but habit depends on more than two idealized variables, and the alternation of plate and column habits with temperature remains mechanistically open, addressed through surface-kinetics models [11]. **The diagram is settled; the explanation is not.**

**Reducible.** Habit class from temperature and supersaturation, to the diagram's accuracy. Onset of branching. Dendrite tip speed and radius. DLA fractal dimension — universal.

**Irreducible.** The specific crystal — two grown side by side are not identical, because growth amplifies fluctuation. Sidebranch statistics, not given by tip theory. Symmetry: real crystals are often strikingly six-fold although the arms grow independently, and whether local rules reproduce the observed *degree* of symmetry is genuinely open.

**The architectural point.** A hexagonal or triangular lattice here is **not an exotic exception** — it is evidence that Grid World must not mean square grid (framework §6). Six-fold symmetry is crystallography, so the anisotropy is physical rather than artifact. This is the one Lab in the catalog where lattice anisotropy is a feature, and it establishes the general requirement.

**Cell state.** *Persistent:* ice or vapour state, attached mass, boundary-layer water. *Derived:* local vapour gradient, attachment probability.

**Assessment.** *(judgment, no standing)* Superb fit, excellent validation, high rediscovery risk, very low practical need. That combination is exactly why it is valuable — as an **integrity test**. This is where beautiful output is most obviously not evidence, and a platform that can run it and say *"this is a lovely picture and it tells you nothing the Nakaya diagram did not"* has demonstrated the discipline §12 and §26 demand on the hardest case. **Benchmark and honesty Lab; not a flagship science opportunity.**

## Lab 11 — Cellular Convection

| | |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Role** | Cross-Lab transfer · architecture stress test |
| **Catalog status** | **Scope reduction recommended** — see below |
| **Standing** | Ungraded |
| **Abstraction level** | **A convective cell, kilometres across — not a fluid element.** This is the whole argument. *Lost at this level:* the fluid dynamics that generate the cell in the first place. |
| **Falsifiable question** | Does a rain-driven excitable-medium mechanism reproduce the observed open-cell oscillation period and transition statistics? |
| **Mechanism fit** | Good **at the cell level**, poor at the fluid-element level |
| **Validation class** | **Direct observational** — daily global satellite imagery since 2000 |
| **Rediscovery risk** | Medium |
| **Practical need** | High; strong incumbent |
| **Reach** | Neighbour-local at the cell level |
| **Topology** | Fixed |
| **Drivers** | Large-scale meteorology as **external input**; precipitation feedback as **interactive mechanism** |
| **Visual credibility** | **Class 1** — mistakable for a climate feedback claim |

**The phenomenon.** Heat a fluid layer from below and past a threshold it stops conducting and starts overturning in organized cells. In marine stratocumulus this produces **mesoscale cellular convection** — honeycomb cloud decks tens of kilometres across, visible from orbit. **Closed cells** have cloudy centres and high albedo; **open cells** have clear centres and low albedo. The same air mass holds both, with sharp boundaries.

Drizzle drives the transition. Rain depletes the cloud, evaporative cooling generates outflow, and the outflow lifts air at cell edges — sustaining the rim while the centre clears.

**The established shortcut.** Convection begins above a critical Rayleigh number, and the initially selected cell size is close to the layer depth — classical linear stability [11]. Cloud albedo from cloud fraction and thickness is arithmetic. Bulk heat flux scaling with Rayleigh number is well characterized.

**The precedent that makes the cell-level framing defensible.** Feingold, Koren, Wang, Xue and Brewer (2010) used satellite imagery and numerical models to show that precipitation-induced downdrafts produce an open cellular pattern that **oscillates between metastable states**, forming a self-organizing cloud field as new patterns replace old ones [5]. That is an excitable/oscillator description of a real cloud field, from the domain's own literature.

**Reducible.** Onset threshold. Initial cell aspect ratio. Nusselt–Rayleigh heat flux relations. Cloud albedo from cloud properties.

**Irreducible.** Open/closed transitions — which state a deck occupies and when it flips, given both can persist under similar large-scale conditions. Pattern defects and their coarsening. Precipitation feedback timing, where the delay is what makes the system oscillate rather than settle. Cell propagation, as open cells trigger neighbours through outflow.

**Scope reduction, adopted from the critique.** The title "Cellular Convection" suggests the Lab covers convection generally. The honest local-mechanism case is much narrower: **mesoscale open/closed cellular cloud organization treated at the convective-cell level.** Classical Rayleigh–Bénard convection is a background and benchmark regime, not the product target. The Lab should be renamed or explicitly split into two declared subdomains before it is built.

**The cross-Lab point, and it is the family's most important idea.** At the cell level this is an excitable medium with a refractory period after rain — the same abstract mechanism as cardiac tissue (entry 21) and catalytic surfaces (entry 35). **If a cloud Study retrieves a mechanism first discovered in a cardiac Lab, that is cross-domain leverage no conventional domain simulator offers.** This is stated as a falsifiable platform hypothesis in the family findings below, not as a hope.

**Cell state.** *Persistent:* cloud liquid or thickness, moisture, sub-cloud energy, precipitation state, recovery timer. *Derived:* albedo, triggering state.

**Assessment.** *(judgment, no standing)* **Plausible and strategically valuable, especially for cross-Lab retrieval. Not an early domain flagship unless the scope is narrowed.** The incumbent — large-eddy simulation resolving the actual turbulence — is strong and trusted for the reducible questions, and grid spacing here carries physical meaning rather than being free.

## Lab 12 — Vegetation Banding

| | |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Role** | Mechanism-supply · early-warning Study Lab |
| **Catalog status** | Standalone Lab |
| **Standing** | Ungraded |
| **Abstraction level** | A patch of ground, metres across, holding plant cover and soil water. *Lost at this level:* individual plant demography and root architecture. |
| **Falsifiable question** | Which spatial early-warning indicators remain reliable across mechanism families and noise histories? |
| **Mechanism fit** | Strong — the directional runoff mechanism gives real physical locality |
| **Validation class** | **Direct observational** — aerial and satellite imagery across multiple continents, Landsat back to the 1970s |
| **Rediscovery risk** | Medium — onset is established, transitions are not |
| **Practical need** | High |
| **Reach** | Neighbour-local, **directionally asymmetric** |
| **Topology** | Fixed |
| **Drivers** | Rainfall as **external input**; water redistribution as **interactive mechanism** |
| **Visual credibility** | Class 1 if framed as desertification risk |

**The phenomenon.** In semi-arid landscapes vegetation does not thin evenly as rainfall declines — it organizes into regular stripes of dense cover alternating with bare ground, tens to hundreds of metres apart, running along contours and often migrating slowly upslope. Plants improve infiltration beneath them, so water entering a vegetated patch stays there and the ground downslope is starved: short-range facilitation, long-range competition. As aridity increases the sequence is consistent and observed — uniform cover, gaps, labyrinths, isolated spots, bare desert.

**The established shortcut.** **Klausmeier (1999)** gave the canonical minimal model: plant biomass with local growth and mortality, water with rainfall input and downslope flow, and uptake concentrated under plants. From two equations it produces banded vegetation on slopes. Crucially, the paper distinguishes two pattern classes by mechanism — **regular patterns from a Turing-like instability, irregular mosaics on flat ground from ecological dynamics amplifying small-scale topographic variability** [6]. That distinction is worth carrying: they are different mechanisms, not variations of one.

Linear stability gives the spacing at onset, the existence condition, and the migration direction analytically.

**Reducible.** Band wavelength at onset. Whether patterns form at all given rainfall, slope, and plant parameters. Migration direction and approximate speed. The ordering of the morphology sequence with aridity.

**Irreducible.** Multistability and history — in much of the parameter range several patterns are simultaneously stable, so the landscape's current pattern encodes how it got there rather than only where it is. Hysteresis: losing vegetation at one rainfall and regaining it at the same rainfall are not symmetric. Defect dynamics during transitions, which is exactly what an early-warning indicator would need to detect.

**Correction from the first pass.** The first pass claimed the early-warning debate sits "entirely" on the irreducible side. That is too strong — some early-warning quantities and transition properties are analyzable through bifurcation theory, stochastic theory, and reduced models. The defensible position:

> **Operational reliability of spatial warning indicators, across heterogeneous mechanisms and histories, requires repeated simulation and empirical validation.**

That is enough, and it is much harder to attack.

**Cell state.** *Persistent:* plant biomass or cover, soil water, infiltration capacity. *Static:* slope. *Derived:* local runoff.

**Assessment.** *(judgment, no standing)* **Strong candidate; probably deserves promotion after fit review.** The mechanism is genuinely local and physically grounded, the reducible core is well characterized so the contribution boundary is knowable, and the live controversy sits in the irreducible remainder. The negative space is unusually valuable: recording which mechanism families produced *no* reliable warning indicator is directly useful to a contested literature, and nobody publishes that.

Blocked in part on **DEC-1** — water flow is arguably the primary mechanism rather than a forcing — though a prescribed rainfall series is cleanly an external input.

## Lab 13 — Plankton Patchiness

| | |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Role** | **Negative fit demonstrator** |
| **Catalog status** | **Merge or demotion candidate** — see below |
| **Standing** | Ungraded; I would grade it **weak** in its ambitious form |
| **Abstraction level** | **Unresolved, and that is the problem.** A fixed surface patch, a moving water parcel, and an organism population are three different causal levels with three different answers. |
| **Falsifiable question** | Does local excitability produce travelling blooms, and does limited dispersal permit coexistence that mean-field forbids? *(Note this asks nothing about the ocean.)* |
| **Mechanism fit** | **Poor** in the ambitious form |
| **Validation class** | Qualitative only for the mechanism; satellite ocean colour cannot separate biology from advection |
| **Rediscovery risk** | Low |
| **Practical need** | Real, but not addressable at this abstraction |
| **Reach** | Neighbour-local — **on a substrate that moves** |
| **Topology** | Fixed grid over a **moving medium**: the core mismatch |
| **Drivers** | Advection as **external input** it cannot represent adequately |
| **Visual credibility** | **Class 1** — model output will resemble ocean colour without sharing a mechanism |

**The phenomenon.** Plankton occurs in patches from centimetres to hundreds of kilometres, and the patchiness is not simply inherited from the physical environment. Two questions have driven the field: why patchiness survives turbulent mixing, and how so many species coexist on few limiting resources — Hutchinson's paradox of the plankton (1961), for which patchiness is one candidate answer.

**The established shortcut.** There is a **minimum patch size with a formula**: the KiSS result — from Kierstead and Slobodkin (1953) and Skellam (1951) — gives the critical patch radius below which diffusive loss exceeds growth and a bloom cannot persist, originally developed for red tide outbreaks [7][8]. Bloom timing follows from classical mixed-layer arguments. Tracer variance spectra under known stirring are standard physical oceanography.

**Irreducible, in principle.** Excitability producing travelling and spiral bloom structure. Coexistence maintained by local competition and limited dispersal. Spatial predator–prey pattern formation, which the well-mixed equations do not produce.

**Why the Lab fails in its ambitious form.** The dominant source of observed structure is probably not the mechanism SCR would model. Large-scale patchiness is substantially **advection** — the ocean stirring a tracer. If the medium itself moves, then adjacency changes, the thing carrying state moves, external flow can dominate local biological interaction, vertical gradients may control everything, and apparent spatial pattern may be entirely inherited from transport.

A fixed grid of ocean surface patches and a set of moving water parcels are **fundamentally different models**, not an implementation choice. SCR could someday support advected Cells; adding that capability would meaningfully broaden the platform's identity and is a DEC-24 expenditure, not a Lab decision.

**The disposition, adopted from the critique.** The salvage version — a generic local excitability and spatial-coexistence Lab that happens to use plankton vocabulary — is legitimate but no longer justifies the title. It probably belongs as **a plankton profile inside Excitable Media (entry 21), or as an ecology example inside a broader spatial-coexistence Lab, or not as a standalone Lab at all.**

That is not a failure of the brief. **It is what a fit review is supposed to conclude**, and this entry is the most valuable weak Lab in the family because it demonstrates SCR will reject a seductive visual domain (§30, closing).

**Cell state.** *Persistent:* phytoplankton and zooplankton biomass, one or two nutrients. *Derived:* growth rate, grazing pressure. The state is not the difficulty.

**Assessment.** *(judgment, no standing)* **Grade weak in its ambitious form. Consider merge or demotion rather than standalone build.**

## Family findings

### The cross-Lab transfer hypothesis

This family produced the strongest platform idea in the catalog so far, and it deserves stating as something testable rather than as an aspiration:

> **SCR should be able to retrieve mechanisms across Labs by structural and observed behavioural similarity, even when subject vocabulary differs entirely.**

That is falsifiable and a Study can test it. **If Search only finds cloud mechanisms when asked about clouds, the semantic Corpus is much less interesting than claimed.** If a cloud Study retrieves an excitable-medium mechanism first recorded in a cardiac Lab, that is genuine cross-domain leverage and no conventional domain simulator offers it.

The Lab catalog is producing a mechanism taxonomy without having been written as one. That is a good sign and it should be captured deliberately.

### On bistability, stated precisely

Three entries across Families B and C revolve around bistability, and the catalog should be exact about what is on offer:

> **Showing that two stable states exist is usually reducible. Localizing where a system flips, how the front moves, and whether spatial coupling preserves the bistability at all — that is the interesting part.**
